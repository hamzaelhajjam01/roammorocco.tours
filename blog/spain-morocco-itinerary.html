From Al-Andalus to the Medina: The Ultimate 14-Day Spain & Morocco Itinerary

How to trace 800 years of shared Moorish civilization from the courtyards of Seville and Granada, across the Strait of Gibraltar, to the blue alleys of Chefchaouen, medieval Fes, and the Sahara dunes.

Published: September 28, 2026 Author: Hamza & Abdelhakim (Roam Morocco Co-Founders) Reading Time: 13 Minutes (2,800 Words) Focus: Spain & Morocco 2-Week Route

There is a moment on the morning ferry from Tarifa, Spain when Europe and Africa exist in the same breath. Stand on the aft deck with a warm cortado in hand: behind you, the whitewashed ramparts of Andalusia recede into the morning haze; ahead, just nine nautical miles across the wind-whipped waters of the Strait of Gibraltar, the green hills of Morocco rise out of the Atlantic.

For eight centuries, Southern Spain and Morocco were not two separate worlds divided by a geopolitical boundary—they were a single, luminous civilization known as Al-Andalus. The intricate cedar wood carvings of the Alhambra in Granada speak the exact same geometric mathematical language as the Medersa Bou Inania in Fes. The olive oil traditions of Andalusia mirror the groves of Meknes. Even the blue doors of Chefchaouen were painted by Jewish and Muslim families fleeing the Reconquista, carrying the heavy iron keys to their lost Andalusian courtyards in their coat pockets.

If you have two weeks for vacation, combining Spain and Morocco in 14 days is the most culturally rewarding journey on the Mediterranean. In this guide, we lay out the exact, stress-free route: how to link Spain's high-speed rail network with a seamless private chauffeur in Morocco, avoiding luggage friction, border confusion, and tourist burnout.

Curator's Philosophy // Hamza & Abdelhakim

“Most travelers make the mistake of treating Spain and Morocco as two disconnected vacations stitched together with a budget flight. But when you cross the Strait by sea and step directly into Tangier with your own local Berber driver waiting at the terminal gates, you feel the living continuity of history. You are not just sightseeing; you are following an ancient caravan of poets, architects, and scholars.”

14-Day Route Overview: Pacing & Logistics

The golden rule of a two-week Spain and Morocco itinerary is not to over-cram. We allocate 6 days to Southern Spain (utilizing Spain's world-class 300 km/h Renfe AVE bullet trains) and 8 days to Morocco (with a dedicated private chauffeur in a luxury 4x4 Prado, giving you door-to-door luggage handling and unhurried desert exploration).

Part 1: The Spanish Chapter (Days 1 to 5)

Seville: The Heart of Andalusia (Days 1–2)

Fly into Madrid Barajas (MAD), hop directly onto the sleek Renfe AVE bullet train from Madrid Atocha, and in two and a half hours, you will disembark in sunny Seville.

Seville is orange blossoms, ceramic tiles, and the strum of flamenco guitars echoing in dimly lit tabernas. The undeniable highlight here is the Real Alcázar—a royal palace built by Christian monarchs using Muslim Moorish artisans (known as Mudéjar architecture). Stare up at the dome of the Ambassadors' Hall: the geometric cedar lattice and gilded plasterwork are the direct ancestors of what you will experience five days later in the riads of Marrakech.

Cordoba & Granada: The Great Architectural Masterpieces (Days 3–4)

On Day 3, take a brief 45-minute AVE hop to Cordoba. Store your bags at the station locker and walk across the Roman Bridge to the Mezquita-Catedral. Walking through the forest of 856 red-and-white double-striped jasper and onyx arches is a transcendent spiritual experience. The prayer niche (the mihrab) was built with gilded Byzantine mosaics sent as a diplomatic gift by the Emperor of Constantinople to the Caliph of Cordoba.

Continue by late afternoon train to Granada, nested at the foot of the snow-crowned Sierra Nevada. Reserve your tickets for the Alhambra months in advance. As late afternoon sunlight hits the Court of the Lions, you will understand why the last Moorish Sultan wept as he surrendered this fortress in 1492.

Part 2: Crossing the Strait of Gibraltar (Day 5 to 6)

The Tarifa to Tangier Ferry: What Nobody Tells You

Many guidebooks suggest taking a flight from Seville or Madrid to Marrakech. Do not do this. Flying robs you of the single most romantic transition in global travel.

Instead, transfer down to the charming coastal town of Tarifa. Tarifa is where the Atlantic Ocean meets the Mediterranean Sea. From Tarifa Port, board the FRS / DFDS high-speed catamaran.

Essential Ferry Tip // Onboard Customs

As soon as you board the ferry in Tarifa, do not sit down immediately. Walk directly to the onboard Moroccan passport control police desk located in the center passenger lounge. Your passport must be stamped by the Moroccan officer while the ship is underway. If you wait until docking in Tangier, you will be delayed at the gate.

Sixty minutes later, the ship glides into Tangier Ville Port. This is critical: make sure you take the ferry to Tangier Ville (which docks right at the foot of the historic medina), and NOT to Tanger Med (which is a commercial cargo container port 50 kilometers away).

Part 3: The Moroccan Odyssey (Days 6 to 14)

Meeting Your Roam Morocco Chauffeur at Tangier Port

Stepping off the gangway into Tangier can feel intimidating if you are on your own with luggage. But when you travel with Roam Morocco, your dedicated English-speaking Berber chauffeur is waiting right outside the arrivals doors holding a personalized name sign.

Your heavy bags are loaded into our air-conditioned Toyota Prado TX 4x4, cold bottled water is handed to you, and the anxiety evaporates. For complete transparency on vehicle types, licensing, and pricing benchmarks, explore our detailed breakdown of Morocco private driver costs.

Chefchaouen to Fes: Mountain Rif to Medieval Heart (Days 6–8)

Drive up into the emerald limestone folds of the Rif Mountains to spend the night in Chefchaouen. Because you have a private driver, you arrive after the big tour buses have left for the afternoon. Walk through the tranquil powder-blue alleys as local women sweep their doorways with fresh mint branches.

On Day 7, head south toward Fes, stopping at the Roman ruins of Volubilis—where olive oil was pressed for the Roman Empire two millennia ago. In Fes, spend a full day exploring Fes el-Bali with an official local historian. To understand how Fes contrasts with the southern energy of Marrakech, read our deep comparison on Marrakech vs Fes, and learn how to shop respectfully with our guide to souk bargaining etiquette.

Crossing the Atlas into the Golden Sahara (Days 9–11)

This is where having a private 4x4 shines. Leaving Fes, you ascend through the Middle Atlas cedar forests (saying hello to wild Barbary macaques), descend the dramatic palm canyons of the Ziz Valley, and arrive at the towering golden dunes of Erg Chebbi in Merzouga.

Leave your suitcases in the vehicle and take a camel or private 4x4 transfer into our luxury desert bivouac. You will sleep in a spacious canvas tent with real queen-sized beds, private hot-water en-suite bathrooms, and climate control, dining under a celestial canopy of stars.

Dune Comparison: If you are wondering why we choose Erg Chebbi over closer desert areas like Zagora, read our comprehensive guide on Merzouga vs Zagora Sahara tours, and check the best time to visit the Sahara to prepare for seasonal temperatures.

Ait Benhaddou to Marrakech: The Grand Finale (Days 11–14)

Journey through the limestone river clefts of Todra and the winding roads of Dades Valley to reach the iconic fortified ksar of Ait Benhaddou. Film buffs will recognize the mudbrick towers from Gladiator and Game of Thrones.

Cross the dramatic 2,260-meter Tizi n'Tichka pass down into the vibrant Red City of Marrakech. Spend your final days touring the Bahia Palace, relaxing in a traditional riad courtyard with orange blossom water, and experiencing our signature custom private tour service.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best month to do a Spain and Morocco trip?

The absolute best months are April, May, September, and October. During these spring and autumn windows, southern Spain experiences glorious 22°C to 26°C (72°F to 78°F) sunshine, while the Moroccan Sahara is pleasantly warm without the brutal 42°C+ summer heat.

How do I handle money and currency exchange between Spain and Morocco?

Spain operates on the Euro (€), while Morocco uses the Moroccan Dirham (MAD). You can pay for almost everything in Spain with credit cards (Visa/Mastercard). In Morocco, while luxury riads accept cards, cash is essential for souks, tips, and café stops. We recommend withdrawing dirhams directly from bank ATMs (like Attijariwafa or BMCE) once you arrive at Tangier Port.

What should I pack for both Spain and Morocco?

Because you will be transitioning between European cosmopolitan cities and traditional Moroccan medinas, lightweight, modest layers are key. Bring comfortable walking shoes for cobblestones, a breathable scarf, linen shirts, and warm fleece layers for cool Sahara evenings.
